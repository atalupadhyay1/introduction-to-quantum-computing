The one-sentence version

A quantum computer is not a faster classical computer. It is a special purpose device that solves a small family of problems exponentially faster by exploiting superposition and entanglement — and everything else about computing still happens on the classical machine.

The three ingredients of a quantum algorithm

  1. A state — the description of the system, in this course a vector of amplitudes.
  2. A transformation — a unitary matrix (a "gate") applied to that state.
  3. A measurement — the irreversible step that turns amplitudes into numbers you can read.

Every lab in this repository follows the same shape, because every quantum program follows the same shape:

state = initial_state()          # 1. state
for gate in gates:                # 2. transformations
    state = apply(gate, state)
outcome = measure(state)          # 3. measurement

What a quantum computer is good for

Problem Speed-up Why
Factoring integers (Shor) exponential period finding + QFT
Simulating molecules and materials exponential the simulator is the physics
Unstructured search (Grover) quadratic amplitude amplification
Quantum simulation of many-body systems exponential same reason as above

And what it is not good for: sorting, spreadsheet work, rendering video, or anything where a good classical algorithm already exists.
