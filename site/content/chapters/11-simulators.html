The classical photon

The classical simulator keeps the intensity in each polarization direction. Malus's law is applied as

\[ P_{\text{align}} = \cos^2(\theta_f - \theta_p) \]

and the "aligned" state sets \(\theta_p \leftarrow \theta_f\), while the "anti-aligned" state sets \(\theta_p \leftarrow \theta_f + 90°\).

The quantum photon

The quantum simulator picks a single outcome at random, with probabilities given by the squared overlaps. The difference is visible in the cascade:

classical quantum
conditional probability of each stage \([0.5, 1.0, 0.5]\) \([0.5, 1.0, 0.5]\)
cumulative probability \([0.5, 0.5, 0.25]\) \([0.5, 0.5, 0.25]\)

The numbers agree — Malus's law is Malus's law. What differs is the mechanism: the classical simulator splits the intensity and the quantum one collapses.

The entangled pair

The entangled simulator keeps the four joint probabilities \((p_{00}, p_{01}, p_{10}, p_{11})\). After a local rotation of the basis by \(\theta\) the outcome probability is the squared inner product with the rotated basis state.

The prediction is the one you already know:

  • measured in the same basis: only \(00\) and \(11\) occur, each with probability \(\tfrac12\);
  • measured in different bases: the four outcomes each occur with probability \(\tfrac14\) — the correlation is gone.

The second line is the security of quantum key distribution in one sentence: a useful correlation needs a common basis, and the basis is what an eavesdropper has to guess.

Cross-checking the models

Lab L12 runs the qubit model and the photon simulator on the same experiment and compares the numbers. This is the most valuable habit in the course: two independently written models agreeing is real evidence, where one model agreeing with itself is not.

Self-check

  • Can you state the difference between the classical and the quantum photon simulator in one sentence?
  • Can you predict the joint outcomes of an entangled pair measured in two different bases?
  • Do you know why measuring in different bases destroys the correlation?
  • Have you run python labs/L12_simulators/lab.py and seen the two models agree?
