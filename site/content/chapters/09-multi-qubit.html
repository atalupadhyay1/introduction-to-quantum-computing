Section 9 — Multi-Qubit Systems

Course section 9 of 17 · Lab L11 · Quiz 6 questions

What you should be able to do

  • Build the \(2^n\)-dimensional basis of an \(n\)-qubit system.
  • Use the Kronecker product to combine single-qubit states and operators.
  • Read a multi-qubit bitstring in the right order (and spot the trap).
  • Apply CNOT, SWAP, CSWAP and Toffoli by hand.

The state space grows exponentially

\(n\) qubits have \(2^n\) basis states:

qubits basis states state vector length
1 2 2
2 4 4
3 8 8
10 1024 1024
50 \(1.1 \times 10^{15}\) \(1.1 \times 10^{15}\)

A 50-qubit state vector is 16 MB — fine. A 1000-qubit one is 16 TB — hopeless. This is the trade-off the course keeps returning to: a simulator needs memory proportional to \(2^n\), a real device needs gates proportional to the circuit depth.

Tensor products

To build the joint state of two systems, take the Kronecker product:

\[ \lvert\psi\rangle \otimes \lvert\phi\rangle \]

With the standard convention that \(q_0\) is the leftmost qubit, the index of a basis state is

\[ \text{index} = b_0 2^{n-1} + b_1 2^{n-2} + \cdots + b_{n-1} 2^{0} \]

so \(|0\rangle \otimes |1\rangle\) is the second column of the 4×4 identity:

\[ |0\rangle \otimes |1\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \]

Order matters. \(|0\rangle \otimes |1\rangle \ne |1\rangle \otimes |0\rangle\): they are different basis states of the same space. And the ordering of qubits inside the tensor product is a convention — which is precisely where the Q# and Qiskit ordering traps in sections 13 and 15 come from.

Two-qubit gates

Gate Action
CNOT flip the target iff the control is \(\lvert1\rangle\)
CZ flip the phase of the target iff the control is \(\lvert1\rangle\)
SWAP exchange the two qubits
CSWAP (Fredkin) swap the two targets iff the control is \(\lvert1\rangle\)
Toffoli (CCNOT) flip the target iff both controls are \(\lvert1\rangle\)

The CNOT is the workhorse. The SWAP can be built from three CNOTs, and the CSWAP from two CNOTs and one Toffoli. The Toffoli is genuinely three-qubit: it cannot be built from two-qubit gates alone.

Writing a bitstring

You must be explicit about the convention. In this repository, and in most textbooks, the state vector is written left to right in the order \(q_0, q_1, \dots\), so index 5 of a 3-qubit register is the bitstring 101 meaning \(q_0 = 1, q_1 = 0, q_2 = 1\).

Qiskit, by contrast, stores qubit 0 as the least significant bit, so its get_counts() prints bitstrings most-significant-qubit first. Q# prints (q2, q1, q0). All three are the same physics with a different bookkeeping convention, and confusing them is the single most common source of "wrong but plausible" answers in the course.

Self-check

  • Can you write out the four basis states of a 2-qubit system?
  • Can you compute \(|0\rangle \otimes |1\rangle\) as a column vector and explain the index convention you used?
  • Do you know how many Toffoli gates you need to swap two qubits, and why a Toffoli cannot be made from two-qubit gates?
  • Can you state the difference between "the state vector is written \(q_0\) first" and "Qiskit's counts are printed \(q_{n-1}\) first"?
