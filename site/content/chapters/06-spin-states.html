Spin-1/2

A particle with spin \(\tfrac12\) — an electron, a proton, a neutron — has exactly two possible results when you measure the component of its angular momentum along any axis:

  • spin up: \(S_z = +\tfrac12\hbar\)
  • spin down: \(S_z = -\tfrac12\hbar\)

This is the definition of a two-level system, so a spin-1/2 particle is a qubit. By convention:

\[ |\uparrow\rangle \equiv |0\rangle, \qquad |\downarrow\rangle \equiv |1\rangle \]

The Stern-Gerlach experiment

Silver atoms fired through a non-uniform magnetic field split into two beams, not a continuous spread. Before the experiment, one expected a continuous distribution of magnetic moment. The result said: the component is quantised.

The devastating follow-up is Stern-Gerlach on a superposition. Take an atom whose spin is already in a superposition of up and down, and pass it through an SG apparatus oriented along a different axis. You do not get "up and down along the new axis" — you get a third distribution, with \(50/50\) outcomes along the new axis for a spin prepared along \(\hat{z}\).

That is the "quantum" of it. The pre-existing state does not survive the measurement; the apparatus overwrites it with a new one. And the probabilities are the squares of amplitudes in the new basis.

Superposition in the Bloch sphere

A spin-1/2 state in a magnetic field precesses. The full state is

\[ |\psi\rangle = \cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,|0\rangle + e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\,|1\rangle \]

with \(0 \le \theta \le \pi\) and \(0 \le \phi < 2\pi\). The angle \(\theta\) is the polar angle on the Bloch sphere and \(\phi\) is the azimuth:

  • \(\theta = 0\) → \(|0\rangle\) (spin up), the north pole;
  • \(\theta = \pi\) → \(|1\rangle\) (spin down), the south pole;
  • \(\theta = \pi/2\) → the equator, the equal-probability superpositions \(|+\rangle\) and \(|-\rangle\).

Spin versus polarization

Photon polarization Spin-1/2
Basis states H / V up / down
Direct measurement? yes, with a polarizer yes, with a Stern-Gerlach apparatus
Rotation rotate the polarizer rotate the magnetic field
Natural implementation optics semiconductors (qubits)

Both are two-level systems, so the mathematics is identical. Spins are how real hardware implements qubits.
